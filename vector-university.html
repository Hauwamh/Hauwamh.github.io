3-year financial forecast

A driver-based forecast for a £312m university, built from student applications and enrolments through to tuition income, staff costs, operating costs and monthly cash. It answers management's question: can the university stay financially sustainable to FY28/29?

Operating margin: history and base-case forecast

Margin peaked at 5.3% in FY24/25, fell to 2.4% as income growth slowed to 1.5%, and dips into a small deficit in FY27/28.

Tuition income by student category, FY25/26

UK Undergraduate is the largest single category; international students together make up 43%.

Answers for management

  • Costs are outgrowing income. Salary inflation plus FTE growth lifts staff costs 5.9–6.2% a year, while income grows 3.5–5.5%. Staff costs are about 65% of the cost base, so this one line drives margin compression.
  • International recruitment is a concentrated risk. £92.2m of tuition (43%) comes from international students, and International Undergraduate enrolment already fell in FY25/26.
  • Cash is the pressure point. Thin surpluses plus a £10.5–11.5m annual capital programme take reserves to a low of £17.4m in August 2028, well below the £42.0m held at the start of FY23/24.
  • All six sustainability flags are red by FY28/29: margin below 3%, staff cost ratio above 65%, international dependency above 40%, cash cover below 2 months, a deficit year, and cash below its starting level.

Recommendations

  • Phase the capital programme around FY27/28, the forecast deficit year.
  • Phase FTE growth or target pay awards below the modelled 4–4.5%.
  • Set a minimum cash-reserve policy (months of cover) and monitor it monthly, alongside international recruitment KPIs.
