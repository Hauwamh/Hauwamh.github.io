& Fitness Studio

A full three-statement financial model (P&L, cash flow and balance sheet) that takes a hybrid café and boutique fitness concept from an idea to a fundable business case: 60 months, seasonality-adjusted, stress-tested across three scenarios and valued two ways.

What the model shows

  • Viable, but thin in Year 1. EBITDA is only £4.4k in Year 1 and net profit is negative (-£13.3k), so the 3-month working-capital buffer built into start-up costs is essential.
  • Headroom over break-even is modest. Monthly break-even revenue is £15.9k, or about 57 café covers a day against 65 assumed: roughly 8 covers of headroom.
  • Downside risk is real. In the worst case, cash is negative by the end of Year 1 (-£31k) and keeps falling, so the lender would need covenants tied to footfall.
  • Two valuation methods, one range. DCF puts equity value at £233k; EBITDA multiples of 2–4x (UK small cafés and gyms) put it at £394k–£583k because the business builds up net cash by Year 5.
